# EMPLOYEE OWNERSHIP JOURNEY

## Start With the Desired End State

## 1. CORE PRINCIPLE

The Employee Ownership Journey should begin **with the end in mind**.

Before asking the owner how the transaction should be structured, ask:

> **"When this is finished, what would you like your successful outcome to look like?"**

The purpose is to help the owner create a mental picture of the destination.

The platform then uses that destination as the **North Star Outcome** for the remainder of the journey.

The journey becomes:

**Desired End State → Explore What Could Get You There → Professional Review → Refine the Plan → Execute**

---

# 2. WHY THIS COMES FIRST

Business owners may know what they want emotionally and financially without knowing the terminology for accomplishing it.

They may think:

> "I've owned this company for 15 years. I want to retire. I'd like about $3–4 million when we close, I'd like another $50,000 a year for several years, and I want my employees to own the company."

They may have no idea whether that points toward:

* An ESOP
* Direct employee purchase
* Seller financing
* An employee-owned acquisition entity
* Staged ownership
* A combination of structures

The platform should not require them to know.

Instead:

**The owner describes the destination.**

**The platform explores possible roads.**

**Professionals determine which roads are actually viable.**

---

# 3. THE "PICTURE YOUR SUCCESS" SCREEN

The journey should begin with a highly visual, simple experience.

## Header

# Picture the Day You Successfully Transition Your Business

> You don't need to know how the transaction will work yet.
>
> First, tell us what you would like the outcome to look like.

---

# 4. OWNER'S DESIRED PROCEEDS

## Question

### About how much would you like to receive at closing?

Use a **range**, not false precision.

Examples:

**Less than $1M**

**$1M–$2M**

**$2M–$3M**

**$3M–$5M**

**$5M+**

**I don't know yet**

Then optionally:

### Is reaching this range nonnegotiable?

**☐ This is nonnegotiable**

The system stores:

**Desired Closing Proceeds**

as a range with:

* Lower bound
* Upper bound
* Confidence
* Nonnegotiable status

Do not require an exact number during the initial journey.

# 6. WHO OWNS THE COMPANY AT THE END?

This should be one of the most important questions in the initial destination picture.

## Question

### When the transition is complete, who would you like to own the company?

Potential selections:

**All employees**

**Employees + management**

**Management**

**A specific employee group**

**Employee ownership with another ownership participant**

**I'm not sure**

Because ownership can involve multiple groups, this should support **multi-select** and, where appropriate, **percentage allocation**.

---

# 7. OWNERSHIP ALLOCATION

Once multiple groups are selected:

## Question

### How would you ideally like ownership to be divided?

For example:

**Employees:** 60%

**Management:** 30%

**Other:** 10%

Require the total to equal **100%** if the owner chooses to specify percentages.

Allow:

**I don't know yet**

This is a preference, not a proposed legal ownership structure.

The platform should describe it as:

> **Your desired ownership outcome**

not:

> **Your recommended ownership structure.**

# 12. CREATE THE OWNER'S "SUCCESS STORY"

The platform should turn the selections into a simple narrative.

For example:

> **Your Desired Outcome**
>
> You have owned your business for 15 years. You would like to transition ownership to your employees within approximately 18 months. You would ideally receive $3–5 million at closing and continue receiving approximately $75,000–$100,000 per year for 5–10 years. You would like employees and management to own meaningful portions of the company after the transition, while preserving the company's workforce and independence.
>
> Your next step is not to choose a transaction structure. We'll use this desired outcome to explore possible ways to get there.

This is incredibly important.

The owner now has a **story in their head**.

---

# 13. ASK THE OWNER TO CONFIRM THE STORY

Before moving into the analytical part of the journey:

### Does this look like the outcome you're trying to create?

**Yes, continue**

**Edit my outcome**

**Start over**

This creates an explicit checkpoint.

---

# 14. THIS BECOMES THE NORTH STAR OBJECT

The system should create a new core object:

## DesiredOutcome

It contains:

* Closing proceeds range
* Future income amount/range
* Future income duration
* Ownership goals
* Desired ownership allocation
* Owner involvement
* Desired transition date/range
* Preservation goals
* Nonnegotiables
* Preferences
* Flexibilities
* Owner narrative
* Version history

This object is distinct from:

**Owner Objective**

because it represents the owner's **desired end state**, not merely individual goals.

---

# 15. THE JOURNEY NOW WORKS BACKWARD

Once the Desired Outcome exists, the platform begins asking:

> **"What would we need to understand to explore whether this outcome could be achievable?"**

Then the journey becomes adaptive.

For example:

### Desired Outcome

$3M–$5M at closing

↓

Platform needs to understand:

Business size
Cash flow
Debt
Estimated value
Financing possibilities

---

### Desired Outcome

$75K–$100K annual income for 5–10 years

↓

Platform needs to understand:

Seller financing preference
Other possible payment structures
Business cash flow
Debt capacity
Desired liquidity

---

### Desired Outcome

Employees own 60%

↓

Platform needs to understand:

Employee ownership objectives
Management participation
Potential ownership structures
Employee readiness
Professional requirements

---

# 16. THE SYSTEM SHOULD NOT PROMISE THE DESTINATION

The owner's desired outcome is a **target**, not an assurance.

The platform might eventually tell the owner:

> **Your desired outcome requires further analysis.**

or:

> **Some parts of your desired outcome may conflict with other requirements under the current assumptions.**

or:

> **There are several structures worth discussing with your professional advisors.**

It should never tell the owner:

> **"Here's how we're going to make that happen."**

---

# 17. SCENARIOS ARE MEASURED AGAINST THE DESIRED OUTCOME

This is the major advantage of starting with the end.

Every Platform Scenario can now ask:

### How does this scenario compare with your desired outcome?

For example:

| Desired Outcome       | Scenario A               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------ |
| $3M–$5M closing       | Within modeled range     |
| $75K–$100K income     | Potentially compatible   |
| Employee ownership    | Yes, structure-dependent |
| Retirement <18 months | Requires review          |
| Company independence  | Potentially compatible   |

This is far more useful than simply describing an ESOP or seller-financing structure.

---

# 18. WHAT-IF EXPLORATION BECOMES MORE POWERFUL

The owner can then ask:

### What if I lowered my closing target?

or:

### What if I stayed involved another 12 months?

or:

### What if I accepted income for 10 years instead of 5?

The system generates another scenario.

The original Desired Outcome remains intact.

The owner is exploring:

**"What would I have to change to make different paths possible?"**

---

# 19. NONNEGOTIABLES BECOME DESTINATION BOUNDARIES

This is where the previously locked rule becomes especially powerful.

For example:

### Desired Outcome

$3M–$5M at closing

**🔴 Nonnegotiable: at least $3M**

$100K/year for 5 years

**🟠 Strong preference**

Employees own majority

**🔴 Nonnegotiable**

Retire within 18 months

**🔴 Nonnegotiable**

The Scenario Engine now knows the **boundaries of the destination**.

It can explore paths without silently moving the goalposts.

---

# 20. PROFESSIONAL REVIEW PACKAGE

The Desired Outcome should be the first and most prominent section of the Professional Review Package.

## What I'm Trying to Accomplish

> **My desired end state**

Then show:

### Financial Outcome

Closing proceeds
Future income
Duration

### Ownership Outcome

Who should own the company

### Personal Outcome

When and how the owner wants to exit

### Business Outcome

What should be preserved

### Must-Haves

Explicit nonnegotiables

### Flexibilities

Things the owner is willing to change

This lets the professional begin with:

> **"Here's what my client is trying to accomplish."**

before discussing transaction mechanics.

---

# 21. THE JOURNEY'S NEW MASTER MODEL

The application now has a stronger structure:

```text
DESIRED END STATE
       ↓
OWNER OBJECTIVES
       ↓
PRIORITIES
       ↓
NONNEGOTIABLES / FLEXIBILITIES
       ↓
BUSINESS FACTS
       ↓
POTENTIAL SCENARIOS
       ↓
WHAT-IF EXPLORATION
       ↓
PROFESSIONAL REVIEW
       ↓
PROFESSIONAL DETERMINATION
       ↓
REFINED END STATE
       ↓
TRANSACTION PLAN
```

Notice that **Professional Determination comes after the platform has explored possibilities**.

The professional can say:

> "To achieve your desired outcome, I think we should investigate a different structure."

The platform then updates the journey.

---

# 22. THE OWNER SHOULD SEE THE DESTINATION THROUGHOUT THE JOURNEY

The Desired Outcome should remain accessible at all times.

A persistent panel might show:

### Your Destination

💰 $3M–$5M at closing

💵 $75K–$100K/year for 5–10 years

👥 Employee ownership

🏁 Retirement within 18 months

🔴 3 Must-Haves

This becomes the user's **North Star**.

The owner can always click:

**Edit Destination**

---

# 23. PROFESSIONALS SEE THE SAME DESTINATION

When the owner invites a professional, that professional should see:

> **This is what the owner is trying to accomplish.**

Then:

> **These are the scenarios the owner has explored.**

Then:

> **These are the questions the owner wants answered.**

That puts everyone on the same page before professional work begins.

The first question is no longer:

> **"What transaction do you want?"**

It becomes:

**The owner defines the destination.**

**The platform explores the roads.**
