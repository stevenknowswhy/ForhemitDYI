# Brainstorming Brief: Build a Platform That Helps Business Owners Sell Their Business to Their Employees

## 1. Mission

We are exploring the creation of a technology platform that helps small and lower-middle-market business owners transition ownership of their company to their employees.

The core problem is simple:

**Many business owners want to retire, transition out, or monetize their business, but they may not have an obvious buyer. At the same time, their employees may have a strong interest in preserving the company, keeping their jobs, and becoming owners, but they generally do not know how to finance, structure, document, or execute an employee buyout.**

We want to explore whether a software platform can make that process substantially easier, more transparent, more standardized, and more affordable.

The platform should potentially take a business owner from:

**“I want to sell my business to my employees.”**

through:

**readiness → valuation → transaction structure → financing → professional team selection → documentation → closing → ownership transition → post-close support**

The goal of this exercise is **not to immediately build the application**.

The goal is to thoroughly brainstorm, challenge, structure, and validate the concept before development.

# 3. Core Transaction Concept

One potential path is a business owner selling the company directly to employees.

The transaction could potentially be financed through several mechanisms.

### A. Seller Financing

The seller could accept some or all of the purchase price as a seller note.

Example conceptual structure:

* Business value: $5 million
* Employees acquire the business
* Employees or an acquisition entity make an initial payment
* Seller finances the remaining purchase price
* Seller receives principal and interest over time

We want the platform to determine whether this can be structured in a way that provides:

* Predictable seller income
* Employee ownership
* Sustainable debt service
* Adequate working capital
* Appropriate risk allocation
* A clear transition of control

### B. Seller Note + Note Sale

An important part of the concept is that the seller may not necessarily want to hold the note until maturity.

The platform could potentially help the seller:

1. Create a seller-financed transaction.
2. Structure the seller note.
3. Estimate payment streams.
4. Identify potential purchasers of the note.
5. Obtain indicative bids or financing terms.
6. Model the discount required to sell the note.
7. Compare:

   * holding the note,
   * partially selling the note,
   * selling the entire note.

For example, the owner might prefer:

**$3 million cash at closing + $2 million seller note**

but later decide to sell the $2 million note for a discounted amount in order to increase liquidity.

The platform should explore whether a marketplace or structured financing process could facilitate this.

# 5. The Key Product Question

The central product question is:

**Can we turn a complex, fragmented employee-business-buyout process into a guided workflow that a business owner can understand and navigate?**

Today, the process can involve many independent professionals, complicated financing arrangements, valuations, tax considerations, legal documents, fiduciary responsibilities, and regulatory requirements.

We want to explore whether software can act as the **orchestration layer** connecting those pieces.

Think of the product as potentially being:

**TurboTax + marketplace + transaction-management system + financing marketplace + project-management platform for employee business ownership.**

That is only a conceptual analogy, not a required product direction.

9. **Prioritize the business owner's experience.**

12. **Avoid building a giant marketplace before identifying the narrowest valuable workflow.**

14. **Identify conflicts of interest before designing marketplace economics.**
15. **Whenever a proposed feature creates regulatory or fiduciary risk, flag it explicitly.**
