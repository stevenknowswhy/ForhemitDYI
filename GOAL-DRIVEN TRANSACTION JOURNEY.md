# GOAL-DRIVEN TRANSACTION JOURNEY

The first transaction category supported by the platform should be:

**Employee Ownership**

The platform should initially support exploration of multiple employee-ownership structures, including:

* Employee ownership through an ESOP
* Direct employee purchase
* Management buyout
* Employee-owned acquisition entity
* Staged employee ownership
* Other potentially applicable structures

However, **the user should never be required to choose a transaction structure at the beginning of the journey.**

The journey should begin with the **business owner's goals and priorities**.

---

# 1. START WITH THE OWNER'S OBJECTIVE

The first question should be fundamentally human, not financial or legal.

For example:

## What would you most like to accomplish by selling your business?

Present approximately 2–3 choices at a time.

Potential goals might include:

### Get the most cash at closing

"I want to maximize the amount of money I receive when the transaction closes."

### Create income over time

"I'm comfortable receiving some of my proceeds over several years if it creates a better overall outcome."

### Preserve the company and create employee ownership

"My priority is seeing the company continue and my employees become owners."

Additional goals can be introduced progressively rather than overwhelming the user at the beginning.

---

# 2. GOALS ARE NOT MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE

The system should recognize that owners may have multiple priorities.

For example:

> **You want to retire, receive substantial cash now, preserve the business, and create employee ownership.**

The platform should help the owner identify:

**Primary Goal**

**Secondary Goals**

**Important Preferences**

This becomes the foundation for the journey.

---

# 3. THE JOURNEY BRANCHES FROM THE GOAL

The platform then explores what could potentially accomplish that goal.

For example:

## Goal: More Cash at Closing

The system may introduce potential paths involving:

* Greater third-party financing
* Less seller financing
* Different ownership structures
* ESOP-related possibilities
* Direct employee acquisition
* Management-led acquisition
* Other structures that may provide different combinations of liquidity and ownership

The application should explain the trade-offs in simple language.

Example:

> **One possible path may provide more cash at closing, but could require greater financing or create other transaction considerations.**

Then:

**Explore This Path**

or

**Show Me Another Path**

The platform continues the journey without forcing a commitment.

---

# 4. A DIFFERENT GOAL CREATES A DIFFERENT JOURNEY

For example:

## Goal: Income Over Time

The platform may introduce:

* Seller financing
* Seller notes
* Staged ownership
* Different combinations of upfront cash and future payments
* Potential tax considerations
* Other structures that professionals may determine are relevant

The user might then be asked:

### How important is receiving ongoing income?

**Very important**

**Somewhat important**

**I'm flexible**

The next questions are generated from those answers.

---

# 5. TAX CONSIDERATIONS SHOULD BE INTRODUCED CAREFULLY

The platform can help the owner identify that taxation may be an important planning consideration.

For example:

> **Your goal includes maximizing the after-tax value of the transaction. Different transaction structures may produce different tax consequences.**

Then:

**Explore the differences**

or

**Continue**

The platform may model or illustrate potential scenarios where appropriate, but it must clearly distinguish:

**Illustrative scenario**

from

**Tax advice**

and

**Professional tax determination.**

The platform should never imply that a particular structure is definitely the owner's best tax strategy.

---

# 6. THE PLATFORM CREATES A DRAFT TRANSACTION PATH

As the owner answers questions, the system gradually develops a preliminary plan.

For example:

## Your Preliminary Ownership Plan

**Primary goal:**
Retire and maximize cash at closing

**Secondary goal:**
Keep the company employee-owned

**Preferred transition:**
Within 12 months

**Current exploration:**
Employee ownership with significant upfront liquidity

**Potential paths identified:**
ESOP
Direct employee acquisition
Employee-owned acquisition entity

**Financing considerations:**
Third-party financing may be important

**Seller financing:**
Optional / under consideration

This is **not a recommendation or commitment**.

It is a structured representation of the owner's current wishes.

---

# 7. THE PLAN MUST BE PRESENTED AS A DRAFT

This is an essential product concept.

The platform should tell the user clearly:

> **This is your preliminary transaction plan.**

> It is based on the information and preferences you provided and is intended to help you have a more productive conversation with qualified professionals.

> Different transaction structures can have significant legal, tax, financial, regulatory, and fiduciary implications. Your attorney, CPA/tax professional, valuation professional, financial advisor, lender, trustee, and other appropriate specialists should review this plan before you rely on it or make transaction decisions.

The objective is not to weaken the product.

It actually gives the product a valuable role:

**The platform helps the owner articulate what they want before they sit down with the experts.**

---

# 8. CREATE A "PROFESSIONAL REVIEW PACKAGE"

One of the most important outputs of the journey should be a package the owner can give to their professional team.

Potential name:

## My Ownership Transition Brief

It could contain:

### Owner Objectives

* Primary goal
* Secondary goals
* Desired timeline
* Desired liquidity
* Desired income
* Employee ownership objectives

### Business Snapshot

* Revenue
* EBITDA / cash flow
* Employees
* Debt
* Industry
* Ownership
* Other relevant information

### Structures Explored

* ESOP
* Direct employee purchase
* Management buyout
* Employee-owned acquisition entity
* Staged ownership
* Other applicable paths

### Current Preferences

* Desired cash at closing
* Seller financing preference
* Desired transition period
* Continued owner involvement
* Employee ownership preferences

### Questions for Professionals

* What structures are actually feasible?
* What valuation is appropriate?
* What financing is available?
* What are the tax implications?
* What legal structure is appropriate?
* What fiduciary or trustee considerations apply?
* What does the professional recommend investigating further?

### Important Notice

**This document reflects the owner's goals, preferences, assumptions, and preliminary exploration. It is not legal, tax, accounting, valuation, investment, financing, or other professional advice.**

---

# 9. PROFESSIONALS SHOULD BE ABLE TO ANNOTATE THE PLAN

This could become a powerful feature.

The owner sends the preliminary plan to their CPA or attorney.

The professional could respond:

> **"Your goal makes sense, but this structure may not accomplish it for these reasons."**

or:

> **"We should investigate an ESOP before ruling it out."**

or:

> **"Your desired cash at closing may require a different financing structure."**

The platform could then allow the owner to:

**Keep Current Plan**

**Create Alternative**

**Change Goal**

**Ask Professional**

The journey continues.

---

# 10. THE PROFESSIONAL DOES NOT HAVE TO START FROM ZERO

This may be one of the platform's biggest practical benefits.

Today a business owner may sit down with an attorney and say:

> "I think I want to sell my company to my employees, but I don't really know how."

With this platform, they could arrive with:

> **Here is what I want. Here are the structures I've explored. Here are my priorities. Here are the questions I want answered.**

The professional can then spend their time on:

**judgment, validation, structuring, negotiation, and execution**

rather than spending the first several meetings simply figuring out what the owner wants.

---

# 11. THE USER CAN CHANGE THEIR GOAL

The journey remains completely reversible.

For example:

The owner initially selects:

**Maximize cash now**

After exploring the consequences, they decide:

> "Actually, I'd rather have more predictable income and potentially different tax treatment."

They can select:

**Change My Goal**

The platform recalculates the possible paths.

This is critical.

The system should treat the journey as:

**Exploration → comparison → refinement**

rather than:

**questionnaire → fixed answer.**

---

# 12. STRUCTURES APPEAR WHEN RELEVANT

The application should not dump all transaction structures onto the user.

Instead:

### Example

User chooses:

**I want substantial cash at closing.**

The platform explains:

> There are several ownership structures that could potentially accomplish employee ownership, but they differ substantially in financing, tax, governance, and liquidity.

Then it introduces perhaps three possibilities.

The user chooses:

**Explore ESOP**

**Explore Direct Purchase**

**Explore Another Path**

Later, another structure can be added if new information makes it relevant.

This keeps the cognitive load low.

---

# 13. THE APPLICATION IS A "TRANSLATOR"

The platform's role is to translate among three worlds:

### Owner's language

> "I want to retire and have enough money to live comfortably."

### Financial language

> "I need a particular combination of upfront proceeds and future cash flows."

### Professional language

> "Let's evaluate which transaction structures, financing arrangements, valuations, and tax strategies can potentially accomplish those objectives."

The platform creates the bridge.

---

# 14. NEVER CONFUSE A PREFERENCE WITH A PROFESSIONAL CONCLUSION

The system should maintain a distinction between:

### Owner says

> "I want the most money possible at closing."

### Platform calculates

> "Under these assumptions, this scenario produces approximately X in upfront proceeds."

### Professional determines

> "Given the actual facts, legal requirements, tax circumstances, valuation, financing, and transaction structure, this approach may or may not accomplish the owner's objectives."

These are three different things.

The platform should keep them separate throughout the workflow.

---

# 15. THE CORE PRODUCT LOOP

The entire experience should follow this sequence:

**Goal**

↓

**Preferences**

↓

**Questions**

↓

**Potential Paths**

↓

**Scenario**

↓

**Trade-offs**

↓

**User Adjusts**

↓

**New Scenario**

↓

**Preliminary Plan**

↓

**Professional Review**

↓

**Professional Feedback**

↓

**Revised Plan**

↓

**Transaction Preparation**

↓

**Execution**

This is the heart of the application.

---

# 16. NORTH STAR

The platform should not attempt to tell the owner:

> **"This is the transaction you should do."**

Its role is to help the owner reach:

> **"This is what I want to accomplish, these are the paths I want to investigate, these are the trade-offs I understand, and this is the plan I want my professionals to review."**

That distinction should be embedded in the product, data model, user interface, and language used throughout the application.

---

# 17. THE FIRST VERSION OF THE JOURNEY

The initial employee-ownership journey should therefore begin with:

### STEP 1

**What are you trying to accomplish?**

### STEP 2

**What matters most about the proceeds?**

### STEP 3

**How quickly do you want to transition?**

### STEP 4

**How important is employee ownership?**

### STEP 5

**How involved do you want to remain?**

### STEP 6

**What do you know about your business today?**

### STEP 7

**Explore potential structures**

### STEP 8

**Compare scenarios**

### STEP 9

**Build preliminary plan**

### STEP 10

**Prepare Professional Review Package**

Only after this point should the platform move deeper into the actual transaction process.

---

# FINAL PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

**The owner begins with a goal, not a financial product.**

The platform then guides them through a small number of understandable choices, shows potential consequences, allows them to change direction, and produces a structured preliminary plan for professional review.

**The platform helps the owner figure out what they want to ask for.
The professionals determine what can and should actually be done.**
