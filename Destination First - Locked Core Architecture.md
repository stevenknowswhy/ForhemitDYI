Locked. 🔒
**Desired Outcome / Destination** is now the **first object created by every journey** and the persistent **North Star** for the entire application.
The core architecture is now:
```text
DESIRED OUTCOME / DESTINATION
↓
OWNER OBJECTIVES
↓
PRIORITIES
↓
NONNEGOTIABLES + PREFERENCES
↓
BUSINESS FACTS
↓
PLATFORM SCENARIOS
↓
WHAT-IF EXPLORATION
↓
PROFESSIONAL REVIEW
↓
PROFESSIONAL DETERMINATION
↓
REFINED DESTINATION
↓
TRANSACTION EXECUTION
```
### The Destination object
It will capture the owner's desired:
**Financial outcome**
* Closing proceeds range
* Future income amount
* Future income duration
**Ownership outcome**
* Who owns the company
* Desired ownership allocation
**Personal outcome**
* Retirement timing
* Desired post-sale involvement
**Business outcome**
* Employees retained
* Culture preserved
* Independence
* Other preservation goals
**Decision characteristics**
* Nonnegotiables
* Strong preferences
* Flexible preferences
* Things the owner wants to avoid
**History**
* Version
* Date created
* Changes
* Previous destinations
### Most importantly
The Destination is **not a prediction, recommendation, or promise**.
It represents:
> **“This is what success looks like to me.”**
The platform then works backward and asks:
> **“What do we need to understand to explore whether there are viable ways to reach that destination?”**
That gives us a very elegant product philosophy:
**Destination first. Complexity second.**
And it means every future journey we create can use the same underlying architecture, even when the transaction type changes.
