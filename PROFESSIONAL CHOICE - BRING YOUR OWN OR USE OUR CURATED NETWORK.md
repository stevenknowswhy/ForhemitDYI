# PROFESSIONAL CHOICE: BRING YOUR OWN OR USE OUR CURATED NETWORK

A fundamental product principle is:

> **The platform should make it easy for the business owner to use the people they already trust, while also making it easy to discover vetted professionals and capital providers through the platform.**

The platform should never artificially force a user to use our professionals.

At every major professional-services decision, give the user two primary paths:

### Option 1: Use My Own Professional

### Option 2: Choose From Our Curated Professionals

The user can change this decision later where practical.

---

# 1. USER-OWNED PROFESSIONALS

The owner may already have:

* An attorney
* CPA
* Financial advisor
* Business valuation firm
* Banker
* SBA lender
* ESOP consultant
* Trustee
* Other transaction professionals

The platform should allow the user to invite those professionals into the transaction.

The owner enters basic information such as:

* Name
* Company
* Role
* Email
* Phone
* Website

The professional receives an invitation to join the transaction workspace.

The platform then assigns the professional the appropriate role and permissions.

The goal is:

**“Bring your own team.”**

---

# 2. CURATED PROFESSIONAL NETWORK

Alternatively, the owner can select:

**“Show me professionals from the platform.”**

The platform presents a small number of relevant choices rather than an enormous directory.

For example:

### Choose a Valuation Professional

**Professional A**

* Firm
* Location
* Years of experience
* Relevant transaction experience
* Typical transaction size
* Industries served
* Credentials
* Fees or fee structure
* Website
* Contact information

**Professional B**

**Professional C**

The user can:

**View Profile**

**Select**

**Compare**

**Choose Someone Else**

The marketplace should emphasize relevant qualifications and transparency rather than simply ranking providers.

---

# 3. CURATED DOES NOT MEAN UNDISCLOSED

The platform should clearly explain why a professional is included.

Potential criteria could include:

* Verified credentials
* Relevant experience
* Transaction volume
* Employee-ownership experience
* Geographic coverage
* Industry experience
* Professional references
* Insurance where appropriate
* Licensing where applicable
* Platform verification
* Availability

The platform should also disclose whether the professional pays the platform for:

* Leads
* Listing
* Referrals
* Transactions
* Other services

This is particularly important because the platform may otherwise create conflicts of interest.

---

# 4. THE SAME MODEL APPLIES TO CAPITAL PROVIDERS

The **Bring Your Own / Curated Network** model should also apply to financing.

For example:

### Financing

**Use My Lender**

or

**Show Me Financing Options**

The platform can then present appropriately matched:

* Banks
* SBA lenders
* Credit unions
* Specialty lenders
* Private lenders
* Other qualified capital providers

The platform should explain the differences in plain language.

---

# 5. SELLER-NOTE BUYERS

Seller-note liquidity should receive its own dedicated marketplace experience.

When the user selects seller financing, the platform should eventually be able to ask:

### What would you like to do with the seller note?

**Keep the note**

**Explore selling part of it**

**Explore selling all of it**

If the user chooses to explore selling the note, the platform presents a curated group of **active seller-note buyers**.

---

# 6. CURATED SELLER-NOTE BUYER DIRECTORY

The platform should maintain a structured database of active note purchasers.

Each buyer profile should contain detailed, useful information.

Potential fields:

### Company Information

* Company name
* Company type
* Headquarters
* Geographic coverage
* Years active
* Assets under management, where publicly available and appropriate
* Investment focus

### Seller-Note Activity

* Types of seller notes purchased
* Typical transaction size
* Minimum purchase size
* Maximum purchase size
* Industries considered
* Geographic limitations
* Preferred note duration
* Preferred interest rates
* Secured vs. unsecured preferences
* Senior vs. subordinated debt preferences
* Whole-note vs. partial-note purchases
* Performing vs. distressed notes
* Other underwriting criteria

### Transaction Information

* Contact name
* Contact title
* Email
* Phone
* Website
* Submission process
* Expected response process
* Required information
* Typical transaction timeline

### Transparency

Where information is available and appropriate:

* Verified by platform
* Date profile was last verified
* Number of transactions completed through the platform
* Relevant transaction history
* Typical transaction sizes
* Seller feedback
* Disclosure of platform fees or relationships

The goal is not merely to show a list of names.

The user should be able to understand:

> **“Who are these people, what do they buy, how do they operate, and could my note fit what they're looking for?”**

---

# 7. MATCH THE NOTE TO THE BUYER

This should eventually become more sophisticated than a directory.

Once the platform has information about the seller note, it should identify potentially relevant buyers based on the note's characteristics.

For example:

### Your Note

**$1.8M remaining balance**

**7.5% interest**

**6.5 years remaining**

**Performing**

**Secured**

**Business EBITDA: $1.2M**

The platform could then say:

> **We found several buyers whose stated criteria appear compatible with this note.**

Then present perhaps **2–3 initial options**.

The user can:

**Review Buyers**

**Request Offers**

**Keep My Note**

---

# 8. DO NOT REPRESENT A MATCH AS AN OFFER

The platform should clearly distinguish among:

**Potential Match**

**Expression of Interest**

**Indicative Terms**

**Formal Offer**

**Accepted Offer**

**Closed Transaction**

A buyer appearing in the application does not mean that the buyer has agreed to purchase the note.

This distinction should be built into the UX.

---

# 9. REQUEST MULTIPLE OFFERS

Where legally and operationally appropriate, the platform could allow the seller to request interest from multiple curated buyers.

For example:

### Your Seller Note

Potential buyers contacted: **4**

Responses: **3**

Then show:

| Buyer   | Indicative Price | Structure        | Status     |
| ------- | ---------------: | ---------------- | ---------- |
| Buyer A |           $1.62M | Whole note       | Interested |
| Buyer B |           $1.57M | Whole note       | Interested |
| Buyer C |           $1.49M | Partial purchase | Interested |

The platform should present these as **market responses**, not as a recommendation or ranking.

The seller decides what to do.

---

# 10. USER CONTROL IS CENTRAL

At every marketplace stage, the user should be able to say:

**Use my person**

**Show me your people**

**Show me other options**

**Go back**

**Change my choice**

The user owns the decision.

---

# 11. PROFESSIONALS BECOME PART OF THE TRANSACTION WORKSPACE

Once selected, the professional should not simply receive a lead.

They become a participant in the transaction.

For example:

**Transaction Team**

* Seller
* Employee Buyer
* Attorney
* CPA
* Valuation Firm
* Lender
* Trustee
* Note Buyer

Each participant receives:

* Appropriate permissions
* Tasks
* Documents
* Deadlines
* Messages
* Transaction status
* Required approvals

The platform becomes the common workspace connecting the participants.

---

# 12. OUR ROLE VS. THE PROFESSIONAL'S ROLE

The platform should clearly distinguish between:

### Platform

* Guides the user
* Organizes information
* Coordinates workflow
* Provides calculations and scenarios
* Connects participants
* Maintains the transaction record
* Provides marketplace access

### Professional

* Provides professional advice
* Performs regulated functions
* Creates or reviews professional documents
* Makes professional determinations
* Performs fiduciary duties where applicable
* Represents the client where applicable

The platform should not pretend to replace professionals where professional judgment or regulated services are required.

---

# 13. THE USER EXPERIENCE

The UX should make this extremely simple.

For example:

## Who would you like to work with?

### Bring My Own

**I already have someone I trust.**

### Show Me Options

**Help me find someone from your network.**

That's it.

The complexity happens afterward.

---

# 14. THE SAME PRINCIPLE FOR EVERY CATEGORY

Use this pattern throughout the application:

**My Professional / Your Network**

**My Lender / Your Financing Network**

**My Valuation Firm / Your Valuation Network**

**My Trustee / Your Trustee Network**

**My Note Buyer / Your Note Buyer Network**

The platform becomes useful whether the user already has a sophisticated advisory team or is starting with nothing.

---

# 15. LONG-TERM NETWORK STRATEGY

Over time, the curated network could become a significant asset of the company.

The platform could develop specialized networks of:

* Employee-ownership attorneys
* Valuation professionals
* SBA lenders
* Banks
* ESOP professionals
* Trustees
* CPAs
* Seller-note buyers
* Closing providers
* Insurance professionals

The value of the network should come from **quality, specialization, transparency, and transaction relevance**, not simply from having the largest directory.

---

# 16. IMPORTANT MARKETPLACE QUESTION

Investigate whether the curated network should be:

### Curated

Small group of highly screened providers.

### Matched

Algorithmically matched based on transaction characteristics.

### Marketplace

Large searchable network.

### Hybrid

A small number of recommended matches plus the ability to browse the broader network.

Determine which model best preserves:

* User trust
* Choice
* Professional quality
* Marketplace liquidity
* Revenue potential
* Regulatory compliance
* Neutrality

---

# 17. DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The platform should never say:

> **“Here is the professional you should use.”**

Instead:

> **“Based on what you've told us, here are several relevant options.”**

The user chooses.

The platform guides the journey, explains the trade-offs, organizes the transaction, and makes the complicated pieces easier to navigate.

**The platform is the navigator, not the owner of the decision.**
